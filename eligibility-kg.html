Eligibility rules as a knowledge graph

Immigration programs share many of the same building blocks: what counts as one year of work, which occupations are skilled, how a language test score maps to a benchmark. I modelled them as a two-layer graph in Neo4j: a shared ontology layer that defines each concept once, and a subgraph per stream that references it. An evaluator then walks a stream's subgraph against a person's profile.

Outcome: each stream is classified as qualified, possible, or disqualified, with the exact missing profile fields that would settle a "possible" and the gaps behind a "disqualified". A missing answer stays unknown instead of being guessed, so the next question asked is always one that changes a result.

MeaningImplicit in question textExplicit condition and concept nodes
Partial evaluationHard: questions don't know what is already knownNatural: check the profile first, ask only for what is missing
ChangeA rule change means editing every copyChange the shared definition once; every stream that references it follows

Anatomy of a requirement

Requirement: one conceptual block of eligibility (work experience, language, education). It can carry its own gate, evaluated first.

Condition: one testable rule. It reads a value through a pointer into the profile, applies an operator, and compares to a literal or to another profile value.

Condition group: combines child conditions and nested groups with AND (default) or OR. Alternatives, such as "English at CLB 7 or French at NCLC 7", become OR groups.

applies_if: a gate on a condition. If it does not apply, the condition is skipped, not failed. Example: a licensing rule only applies to regulated occupations.

A condition as stored on its node: per-ability language minimums, only evaluated when the person has a test result.

When a rule could not be computed from existing profile fields, the profile schema grew a derived field instead of the rule growing code: for example, hours pre-aggregated by country, time window, and occupation level. Business logic stayed declarative and testable.

Try it: one requirement, evaluated

The language requirement of the Canadian Experience Class: an approved test, valid on the day you apply, and a minimum score that depends on the occupation level of your experience. The minimum is an OR group of four conditions, each gated by applies_if.

Group rule: OR is true if any participating child is true; skipped children do not participate; with no true child, any unknown keeps the group unknown. The requirement here reduces like AND: any false → not met, any unknown → unknown, otherwise met.

Around the evaluator

I built the evaluator into a conversational loop. A router turns what the person has said into a partial profile; the evaluator returns qualified, possible, and disqualified streams plus the missing fields; a question recommender picks the next question; answers update the profile, and the loop repeats until nothing unknown can change a result.

The question recommender ranks missing fields by one of three policies: commonality (needed by the most still-possible streams), gating (fields that can disqualify many streams at once, such as occupation level or residence), or discrimination between the remaining streams. Only fields a person can actually answer are asked; derived fields are computed.

Keeping it current

Official pages change. Each stream subgraph records its source URL and extraction date, and I wrote a playbook for authoring and maintaining streams: detect a change (page timestamp, policy notices, archived-snapshot diffs) → localize it to requirement and condition IDs → update while keeping IDs stable when meaning is unchanged → validate against seed profiles covering edge cases and opposite branches → version with a changelog → peer review.
